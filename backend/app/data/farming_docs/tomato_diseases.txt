Yellow leaves on tomato plants are commonly caused by nitrogen deficiency,
overwatering, or poor drainage. Nitrogen deficiency shows as uniform yellowing
starting with older leaves. Overwatering causes yellowing with wilting and
soggy soil. Fix: reduce watering frequency, ensure good drainage, and apply
balanced NPK fertilizer if deficiency is suspected.

Blossom end rot appears as dark sunken patches on the fruit bottom, usually
caused by calcium deficiency or inconsistent watering.

Early blight causes brown spots with concentric rings on lower leaves,
caused by the fungus Alternaria solani. Treat with copper-based fungicide
and remove infected leaves.
